{\bfseries\fontsize{20pt}{28pt}\selectfont Abstract\par}
\vspace{1.1cm}

This project considers energy management for a grid-connected house with local demand, PV and a battery. A network that predicts grid power, charge, discharge and state of charge as independent outputs can still produce an infeasible command, for example simultaneous charge and discharge. The proposed model learns only a signed battery command. Charge, discharge, next state of charge and grid exchange are then obtained from the battery and power-balance equations, so the constraints used in the model remain satisfied.

The data set has 3289 hourly points, from 17:00 on 11 January 2016 to 17:00 on 27 May 2016. The calendar follows a public appliance data set. UCI and NASA POWER were not available in this run, so load and PV for Stambruges were generated from a residential profile and a solar-altitude envelope. Dynamic programming gives the reference schedule. Two MLPs are trained on a 60/20/20 chronological split with five seeds and tested with the model's own state of charge.

The physics-embedded controller has a numerically zero power residual and no violations of the defined constraints. The unconstrained model follows the labels more closely, but about 11.4 percent of its SOC values leave the allowed interval and 35.4 percent of steps charge and discharge together. The physics-embedded operating cost is $42{.}418 \pm 0{.}511$: 6.2 percent below the no-battery case and 2.8 percent above the DP reference. After a safety map the data-driven cost is $42{.}234 \pm 0{.}318$; five seeds are not enough to rank the two. The numbers apply to this household case only.
